About "Reading": a spec sheet is one of two things. A sheet made by a computer holds real text — you could select and copy it in a PDF reader — and this tool reads it exactly and instantly. A sheet that was scanned or photographed is just a picture of a page; nothing can be selected, so the text has to be recognised from the image (OCR). OCR is slower (about 5 seconds a page, and the first page of a session downloads roughly 15 MB of language data) and it is very good but never perfect — most mistakes are a lost decimal point or a mangled superscript.

You do not need to choose. On Automatic each page is read from its real text when it has any, and only pages without any are given to OCR — so a folder holding both kinds is handled correctly without you doing anything. The chip beside the box tells you which kind the open PDF is. Use Force OCR only to test what OCR makes of a page that does have text; use Never to make sure nothing is guessed, at the cost of scanned sheets coming back blank and flagged.

Double-check reads each scanned page a second time at a different size and flags any value the two readings disagree about in orange. It doubles the time on scans, and it is the only dependable way to catch a decimal point that one reading could not see. It does nothing on real-text sheets.
